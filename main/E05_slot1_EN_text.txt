On 23 March 2025, an Istanbul court ordered Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu’s pretrial detention on corruption-related allegations, following his police detention four days earlier. He denied wrongdoing. The order was not a conviction; he was subsequently suspended as mayor.

The decision was politically controversial because İmamoğlu was a leading opposition figure and potential presidential challenger to Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. His Republican People’s Party (CHP) selected him as its presidential candidate that same day. Opposition supporters saw the proceedings as an attempt to eliminate electoral competition, while the government maintained that the judiciary acted independently. The ruling intensified ongoing protests and concerns about judicial independence.

Elected officials are not above the law, and credible corruption allegations warrant investigation. However, pretrial imprisonment requires individualized justification—such as a substantial risk of flight or interference with evidence—and consideration of less restrictive alternatives. Political prominence neither grants immunity nor justifies detention.

Overall, the action was democratically damaging and, without a convincing demonstration that imprisonment was necessary, normatively unjustified. It restricted the liberty of an unconvicted person while disrupting political representation and competition. This assessment concerns detention’s necessity and proportionality, not a determination of İmamoğlu’s guilt or innocence.

On 15 March 2016, a Turkish court ordered the pretrial detention of three Academics for Peace signatories; a fourth, Meral Camcı, was detained after returning to Turkey. The academics had publicly defended a petition condemning civilian suffering and state security operations in the predominantly Kurdish southeast and calling for renewed peace negotiations. Authorities alleged that their statements constituted terrorist propaganda.

The detentions were politically controversial because they treated criticism of government security policy as a potential terrorism offense. Supporters of the prosecution viewed the petition as legitimizing the PKK and unfairly blaming the state amid serious armed violence. Critics saw the arrests as intimidation of dissenting scholars and an attack on academic freedom.

Turkey had a legitimate obligation to protect people from armed attacks. Nevertheless, that responsibility did not remove its obligations to protect peaceful expression, due process, and the presumption of innocence. Pretrial detention required an individualized demonstration of necessity, not merely a terrorism allegation or a court’s authorization.

Overall, the action was disproportionate and politically damaging: the public statements described did not establish a compelling basis for imprisonment. Detaining scholars for nonviolent advocacy chilled debate and narrowed the space for peaceful discussion of the Kurdish conflict.

On 13 January 2025, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan formally launched Turkey’s “Year of the Family,” linking support for marriage and childbirth to declining fertility and population ageing. The initiative included expanded interest-free loans for newlyweds and increased financial assistance for families with children.

The controversy concerned not simply helping families, but the political definition of “family.” Erdoğan accompanied the initiative with rhetoric portraying LGBTQ+ people and movements as threats to family life. Its emphasis on marriage and reproduction reinforced the government’s religious-conservative agenda and raised concerns about women being valued primarily as wives and mothers. Critics also questioned whether financial incentives could address deeper obstacles to family formation, including inflation, insecure employment and inadequate childcare.

Normatively, supporting children and reducing the costs of caregiving are legitimate state objectives. However, assistance should respect reproductive autonomy and equal citizenship rather than stigmatize sexual minorities or privilege one household model. Although largely symbolic, a presidential designation can shape policy priorities and legitimize exclusionary attitudes.

Overall, the declaration had potentially beneficial welfare aims, but its exclusionary framing weakened its legitimacy. A stronger approach would support diverse households, affordable childcare and voluntary parenthood without treating demographic goals as obligations imposed on citizens.

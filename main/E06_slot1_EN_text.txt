On 31 October 2024, Turkey’s Interior Ministry suspended Ahmet Özer, the opposition CHP mayor of Istanbul’s Esenyurt district, following his arrest on allegations of membership in the outlawed PKK/KCK. The ministry appointed Istanbul deputy governor Can Aksoy as acting mayor rather than allowing the elected municipal council to choose a replacement. Özer denied the accusations.

The controversy concerned both the terrorism case and the displacement of voters’ choice only months after the March local elections. CHP leaders described the intervention as politically motivated. Applying a trusteeship practice associated especially with pro-Kurdish municipalities to a prominent Istanbul opposition municipality heightened concerns about selective enforcement and central control over local government.

Investigating credible terrorism allegations is legitimate, and Turkish law provides for suspensions and trustee appointments in terrorism-related cases. However, **formal legality does not by itself establish democratic legitimacy or proportionality**. Allegations are not convictions, while installing a central-government appointee weakens local representation and accountability.

Overall, I assess the action as democratically damaging and insufficiently justified without compelling, independently scrutinized evidence that less intrusive alternatives were inadequate. A fair criminal investigation could proceed while giving greater protection to due process and continuity of elected local representation.

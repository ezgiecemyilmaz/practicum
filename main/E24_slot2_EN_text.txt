On 24 October 2025, following a judicial decision, state-appointed trustees took over TELE1, a television channel known for opposition-oriented coverage. The takeover, administered through Turkey’s Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF), accompanied an espionage investigation involving editor-in-chief Merdan Yanardağ.

The controversy concerned not simply investigating alleged wrongdoing, but transferring an entire broadcaster’s management into state hands. In Turkey’s constrained media environment, this threatened a platform for government criticism, risked chilling other journalists, and could narrow the views available to audiences. Judicial authorization alone does not establish that an intervention is necessary, proportionate, or insulated from political influence.

Investigating genuine espionage is legitimate, and journalists are not exempt from criminal law. However, allegations must be tested through due process, with the presumption of innocence respected. Taking over a newsroom requires especially compelling justification that less intrusive measures would be inadequate, alongside safeguards for editorial autonomy and prompt, effective judicial review.

**Overall, I assess the trusteeship negatively.** Without a demonstrated necessity for institutional control, its foreseeable harms to press freedom and democratic accountability outweigh its asserted investigative purpose. This assessment does not prejudge the criminal allegations: it distinguishes investigating individuals from placing their broadcaster under state control.

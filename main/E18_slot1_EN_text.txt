On 18 November 2017, Ankara’s governorate imposed an indefinite ban on public LGBTQ+ events across the province, including film screenings, exhibitions, performances, and discussions. Authorities cited public security, public order, and the possibility of hostile reactions.

The decision was politically controversial because it restricted a marginalized community’s public presence rather than responding to particular unlawful conduct. Against the backdrop of Turkey’s post-2016 state of emergency and broader restrictions on civil society, critics viewed it as another expansion of executive control over civic space. It also raised concerns that conservative social objections were being converted into state-imposed limits on minority rights.

Protecting people from violence is a legitimate government responsibility. However, an indefinite, category-wide prohibition is difficult to reconcile with freedom of expression, peaceful assembly, equality, and proportionality. Anticipated hostility should ordinarily prompt protection for participants, not give opponents an effective veto over lawful events. The ban also risked stigmatizing LGBTQ+ people as a security problem.

Overall, this was an unjustifiably sweeping and discriminatory measure. Authorities should have used specific risk assessments, protective policing, and narrowly tailored, reviewable restrictions where genuinely necessary, rather than excluding LGBTQ+ activities from public life.
